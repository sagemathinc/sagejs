# Full $\Gamma_1$ benchmark receipt: 2026-09-04

## Contract

Each system constructs the complete rational $q$-expansion basis of
$M_k(\Gamma_1(N))$ through the Sturm bound, then computes the first cuspidal
$T_2$.  Sage.js and Sage also compute a diamond operator.  Language startup
is excluded.  The harness rejects a row unless all three systems agree on the
ambient dimension, cusp dimension, Sturm precision, and $T_2$ trace.

The host was Linux x86-64 on a 16-core AMD EPYC 7B13.  Individual mathematical
tasks are single-threaded.  The runtimes were Node 26.8.1, SageMath
10.9.post30, and Magma V2.18-5.

## High-degree character case

Command:

```bash
GAMMA1_CASES=27:3 node bench/modular/gamma1-spaces/benchmark.cjs --json
```

For $N=27$, $k=3$, all systems returned
$\dim M=69$, $\dim S=39$, Sturm precision $164$, and
$\operatorname{tr}(T_2)=-9$.

| System | full basis | first $T_2$ | diamond |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Sage.js | 3.668 s | 0.865 s | 0.046 s |
| SageMath | 9.299 s | 1.825 s | 1.190 s |
| Magma | 0.630 s | below timer resolution | not exposed by this interface |

Thus Sage.js was $2.54\times$ faster than Sage for the full basis and
$2.11\times$ faster for first Hecke action.  Magma remained $5.8\times$
faster for basis construction.

## Larger-level component profile

Command:

```bash
node bench/modular/gamma1-spaces/profile-components.cjs 53 2
```

At $N=53$, $k=2$, the four character-orbit components had
$(\operatorname{ord}(\chi),[K_\chi:\QQ],\dim_K,\dim_\QQ)$ equal to

$$
(2,1,6,6),\quad(13,12,5,60),\quad(26,12,6,72),\quad(1,1,5,5).
$$

| Orbit | cusp basis | Eisenstein basis | cached cuspidal $T_2$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| order $2$ | 3.045 s | 0.628 s | 0.053 s |
| order $13$ | 5.717 s | 4.699 s | 0.030 s |
| order $26$ | 6.402 s | 4.969 s | 0.037 s |
| principal | 2.784 s | 4.580 s | 0.049 s |

The optimized path accumulates character Eisenstein divisor sums directly in
the cyclotomic power basis, publishes them in one exact batch, and preserves
those coordinates during the scalar combinations used by Hecke-dual
reconstruction.  End-to-end measurements are:

| System | full basis | first $T_2$ | diamond |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Sage.js | 45.804 s | 11.865 s | 0.325 s |
| SageMath | 152.456 s | 151.733 s | 163.298 s |
| Magma | 2.800 s | 0.010 s | not exposed by this interface |

Both agree on $\dim M=143$, $\dim S=92$, Sturm precision $470$, and
$\operatorname{tr}(T_2)=-23$.  Sage.js is now $3.33\times$ faster than SageMath
for the complete basis, $12.8\times$ faster for the first Hecke operator, and
about $502\times$ faster for this diamond operator.  Magma remains about
$16.4\times$ faster for the complete basis; its specialized implementation is
the honest remaining performance target.

## Level-73 scaling observation

At $N=73$, $k=2$, Sage.js constructed the $258$-dimensional basis through
Sturm precision $890$ in $285.789$ seconds, then computed the cuspidal $T_2$
in $32.067$ seconds with trace $-33$.  The matched SageMath process had not
completed the subsequent $T_2$ after 30 minutes and was stopped; the older
Sage driver did not flush its completed basis phase separately, so this is an
end-to-end lower bound rather than a claimed basis ratio.  The driver now
flushes each phase immediately for future long-running receipts.

## Reproduction notes

The benchmark drivers are `sagejs.cjs`, `sagelite.py`, and `magma.m` in this
directory.  Set `GAMMA1_CASES` to a comma-separated list such as
`37:2,53:2`.  Each completed case is printed to standard error immediately so
a later bounded timeout does not discard earlier receipts.

Magma's modular-form parent does not expose one full multi-character Hecke
operator in this interface, so its Hecke phase uses the sign-$+1$ cuspidal
modular-symbol space.  Magma does not expose the matching full-space diamond
operation here.  These differences are reported explicitly rather than
silently treating the APIs as identical.
