The question

Where does cosine meet the diagonal?

Rewrite cos(x) = x as f(x) = cos(x) − x = 0. The endpoint values have opposite signs, and the function is continuous on [0, 1]. That gives a bracketed method the fact it needs: at least one root lies inside.

Automatic planning chooses Brent’s method: it attempts secant and inverse-quadratic steps for speed, while retaining a bisection safeguard. Planning does not call the function; execution and independent validation remain separate records.

Choose with intent

Methods are promises with premises.

The fast method is useful only inside the mathematical situation it assumes.

01

Bisection

Bracketed · derivative-free

Needs

A finite interval, continuity, and opposite endpoint signs (or an endpoint root).

Promises

Preserves the bracket and halves its width every iteration.

Watch: a jump can preserve signs without containing a zero.

02

Brent

Bracketed hybrid · selected

Needs

The same finite, continuous, sign-changing interval as bisection.

Promises

Tries faster interpolating steps and falls back to bracket-preserving steps.

Watch: bracket shrinkage alone does not validate a residual.

03

Secant

Open · derivative-free

Needs

Two distinct estimates and distinct finite function values near a smooth root.

Promises

Often converges faster than bisection near a simple root.

Watch: no bracket is preserved; stagnation and wrong-root convergence are possible.

04

Newton

Open · derivative-based

Needs

A useful starting basin, differentiability, and a nonzero reliable derivative near the root.

Promises

Local quadratic convergence for a simple root under the usual hypotheses.

Watch: cycles, zero derivatives, and divergent steps are normal failure modes.

validation_failed

A shrinking bracket surrounds a jump.

For −1 if x < 0 else 1, Brent reports converged near zero. Yet the residual is exactly 1, so independent validation sets success = false. Continuity—not a sign change alone—was the missing premise.

Action: inspect the function around the candidate. A tighter x tolerance cannot make this residual smaller.

invalid_bracket

Both endpoints have the same sign.

For x² + 1 on [−1, 1], Brent stops after two evaluations. It returns no candidate, and the mathematical conclusion remains indeterminate.

Action: sample or plot first; choose opposite-sign endpoints on a continuous interval. Same signs do not generally prove there are no roots elsewhere.

maximum_iterations

Newton alternates: 0, 1, 0, 1.

For x³ − 2x + 2 from x₀ = 0, a valid derivative still leads to a two-cycle. The bounded run ends after 8 iterations with residual 2.

Action: inspect the trace. If continuity and a sign-changing bracket are available, switch to Brent; otherwise improve the start or the model.

The durable lesson

Stopping is an event. Success is a claim with evidence.

A method can stop because its interval is narrow, its step is tiny, its budget is empty, or its derivative is unusable. Sage.js records that event, then separately asks whether the candidate is finite, its residual meets the declared threshold, and any promised bracket invariant still holds.
